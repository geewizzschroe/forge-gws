## Summary: the findings that matter most

1. **There is no difficulty ladder, and the early game is the wall.** Enemies are picked uniformly at random among those whose `minRow` allows them (`code/RunMap.java:92-104`); only The Restless Dead is gated (row 2). Goblin Raiders, available on row 0, is the hardest normal enemy (hero wins 0 to 21% in the third report; 4 of 48 in my sweep of Jill's four starters) and ended 24 of 50 simulated runs; A Troop of Dwarves is the easiest (21 of 48). Krenko's Warband (boss) was never beaten: 0 of 14 fights in the third report and 0 of 48 more in my sweep of Jill's starters, all from full life. The only boss wins I found are Lightning with unlocks: 1 of 10 at L3 and 3 of 10 at L8 (section 2.4). The boss deck is the Goblin Raiders list plus seven cards (38 of Raiders' 40 card copies appear in it), so the early and final fights look alike. (Section 1.5.)
2. **Healing cannot keep up with damage.** Rest heals 6 of 20 life and appears on 0.78 nodes per random path (43% of random paths have none; 15% of maps have none anywhere). Won fights in the third report cost Jill about 12 life (Dwarves) to 16 (Raiders). The strongest healer is a relic (Healing Herb, about +5 to +10 life per fight by my estimate) that was never obtained in any simulated run. (Sections 4.4, 2.3.)
3. **Gold, relics and the boss reward barely function.** About 107 gold is earned before the boss on an average path, but shop visits are 0.73 per run (18% of maps have no shop), average gold at a shop visit is about 59, a relic costs 60 and card removal 75/150/225. The 100 gold and the relic after the boss do nothing: `floors` is 1, so beating the boss ends the run (`code/Run.java:243-259`). Third-report runs gained 0.0 relics each. (Section 4.5.)
4. **Upgrades are rare, creature-only and mostly small.** My estimate is about 1.8 to 2.3 upgrades in a run that wins every fight (well under one in the short runs the simulator actually produces), from reward options (25% of creature options only, so 11 to 19% of options) and Upgrade nodes (0.6 per random path). Spells and equipment can never be upgraded. DESIGN.md N10 says "reward cards come upgraded 25% of the time"; the code only upgrades creature options (`code/Run.java:446`). Half of the 8-entry pool is marginal (Haste, Vigilance, Trample on cards that often have it or do not need it). (Section 3.)
5. **Meta-progression is paced for a win rate the mode does not have.** XP comes only from won fights (30 each; a loss gives 0, `code/RunConfig.java:67`). Simulated runs earned 9 to 33 XP each, so character L3 (180 XP) takes about 6 to 20 runs, L5 (1,380 XP) about 40 to 150 runs, and account level 2 (500 XP) about 15 to 55 runs. The Jill milestone unlocking Frozen Throne (bounce a boss's commander 30 times) needs 30 boss-commander bounces, but the boss was reached once in 50 simulated runs. (Section 5.)
6. **All Base and relic scripts do what their text says; no script defect found.** I ran every Base, every relic and both Lightning Base-ability unlocks in the engine (section 2). There is no repeat of the `nonToken` problem. Two things are not script defects but matter: the AI never activated Lightning's Guardian Corps Armory in 8 logged games (Equipment was cast in 5 of them) (so Lightning simulations undervalue her Base), and none of the three earlier simulation reports includes any meta-progression unlock.
7. **Base power is very uneven.** Goblin Warren (boss) is a free 1/1-plus-anthem engine every turn, Open Graves is a strong value engine for a deck of 29 Zombies, Wayfarer's Camp is repeatable card draw for all four Jill starters; Mountain Pass (about one Treasure per Dwarf, 12 of 40 cards) and Raider Camp (haste for creatures that often already have it) are small. Lightning's L3 unlock Sunforger gives her a 7/2 first-strike, trample, lifelink commander with a free-spell tutor attached, every time she is cast. (Section 2.)
8. **The map gives little agency.** Two to three nodes per row, 66% of steps offer a single option, a player makes about 2.4 real choices per run. Node types are rolled independently with no guarantee of a Rest, Shop or healing before the boss. (Section 4.2.)
9. **Starters differ a lot on paper, much less in simulation.** Mono-blue "Tides of Ice" (the default) has no removal except bounce and no lifegain; UB has eight kill spells; Frozen Throne is slow (average MV 3.4); Lightning's commander is far stronger than Jill. In my sweep the four Jill starters won 12, 10, 8 and 6 of 48 single fights (mono-blue, UW, UB, Frozen Throne): inside the noise except possibly Frozen Throne, and not in unlock order. All four are walled by Raiders and the boss. (Section 1.4, 1.6.)
10. **Overlapping systems.** The free Cut node and the 75-gold shop removal do the same job; Rest, Healing Herb and Iron Heart overlap; the Green Ramp unlock at account level 3 can almost never appear (it is off-colour for both characters); black has no bucket. (Section 5.)

### 1.3 Hero starters, one by one

**Jill, commander (MV 3, 2/2): ETB bounces up to one other nonland permanent.** She can bounce her own ETB creatures. All four starters use Wayfarer's Camp.

- **Mono-blue "Tides of Ice"** (default, available at level 1). Flash-and-bounce tempo: 8 creature names, 6 instants, 1 sorcery, 1 aura. Curve is 6 / 14 / 0 / 10 / 2 at MV 1 / 2 / 3 / 4 / 5 (copies). Win conditions: flyers (Faerie Seer 1/1, High Fae Trickster 4/2, Skyship Buccaneer 4/3, two Thopters from Whirler Rogue), Moon-Circuit Hacker card flow, and a late Rite of Replication (kicked at 9 mana: five copies of a creature). Interaction: two Counterspells, two Soul Reads (counter or draw two), bounce (Fading Hope, Into the Roil, Run Away Together), and nothing that kills a creature or gains life.
  - Synergy is real: ETB creatures plus Jill's bounce (Faerie Seer, Burrog Befuddler, Micromancer, Whirler Rogue), High Fae Trickster gives flash to Rite of Replication and Starlit Mantle, Micromancer can fetch Fading Hope or Chilling Trap.
  - Weakness (certain from the list, likely in play): it has no answer to a wide board except trading counterspells and bounce one for one; Chilling Trap only draws if a Wizard is in play (8 copies of 32 are Wizards).
  - Fit with theme and Base: the name says "ice", but little in it is icy (Chilling Trap). Wayfarer's Camp at instant speed fits a flash deck that wants to spend spare mana at the opponent's end step.
- **UW "Shimmering Wake"** (character L3). Lowest curve of Jill's decks (18 copies at MV 2 or less), 11 creature names, many with flash (Cathar Commando 3/1, Mischievous Pup 3/1, Resolute Reinforcements, Brineborn Cutthroat). Answers: Stasis Snare x2 (exile, flash), Luminous Rebuke x2 (destroy; costs 2 against a tapped creature), Repel the Darkness x2 (tap two, draw). Top end is only MV 4 (Micromancer, High Fae). Looks like a coherent flash-tempo deck with real answers. Micromancer finds only Chilling Trap here (no other MV-1 instant), weaker than in mono-blue.
- **UB "Drowned Court"** (character L8, 4,680 XP). Most removal: Sephiroth's Intervention x2, Go for the Throat x2, Moment of Craving x2, Serpent Assassin x2, plus Ahriman (flying deathtouch); 10 evasive creatures; Gravedigger value; Skymarch Bloodletter and Sephiroth's Intervention gain life. Highest raw card quality of the five on paper. Double pips (Counterspell UU, Serpent Assassin BB) make the per-turn land choice matter for a human. Interaction with the enemy Base: against The Restless Dead, every creature this deck kills gives the enemy a 2/2 Zombie (section 2.2).
- **Frozen Throne** (milestone unlock, independent of the core). A tap-and-stun lock: 13 of 16 names tap or freeze something (Frost Trickster, Chrome Prowler, Gilded Scuttler, Network Disruptor, Kapsho Kitefins, Ultimecia, Summon: Shiva, plus "doesn't untap" Auras: Capture Sphere, Sleep Magic, Stuck in Summoner's Sanctum). Finishers: Hraesvelgr 5/5 flying vigilance ward 2 (the deck's best card), Ultimecia 4/4 (taps all enemy creatures, draws). It is the slowest deck (average MV 3.38; 8 copies at MV 5 or more; only 8 at MV 2 or less). It does what its theme says. Concern (likely): a one-land-a-turn mode makes a top-heavy lock deck weak against 1 and 2 drop swarms (Raiders), and its Auras are two-for-one targets; my sweep is consistent with this (0 of 12 against Raiders, 2 of 12 against Dwarves, 6 of 48 overall, the lowest of the four) but is too small to prove it. The Base that fits it best, Frozen Bastion, is a different unlock (Jill L5) and the starter itself comes with Wayfarer's Camp.

**Lightning, commander (MV 3, 3/2 first strike, trample, lifelink; "Stagger": combat damage to a player doubles all later damage to that player until her next turn).** One starter, "Army of One".
- 14 creature copies (7 names), 6 Equipment (Dragoon's Lance, Warrior's Sword, Genji Glove), 10 instants, 2 sorceries. Curve is the cheapest of the heroes (10 copies at MV 1, 10 at MV 2). Four Job-select Equipment (Lance, Sword) make a 1/1 Hero token already equipped, which the Armory makes hasty.
- Synergy is strongest here: Equipment plus Item Shopkeep (menace), Weapons Vendor (draw, pay to attach), Slash of Light (counts creatures and Equipment), Freya Crescent (mana for Equipment), Ashe (fetches an artifact; 8 of 32 cards are artifacts, about 79% to see one in the top five), Zack Fair (moves counters and Equipment), Genji Glove (double strike, extra combat).
- Concern (guess, not simulated): Genji Glove or any double strike on Lightning interacts with Stagger so that the second strike is doubled; with Sunforger (+4 power) a single unblocked Lightning could deal 7 then 14. Not verified in play.
- Fit with Base: the deck is the one most built around its Base's trigger (Armory), but Armory only matters when Equipment is in play (6 of 32 cards, more with Sunforger).

### 1.5 Enemy decks and the difficulty ladder

| Enemy | First row | Life / hand | Base gimmick | Deck in one line | Avg MV |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Goblin Raiders | 0 | 20 / 6 | Goblins have haste | 33 of 40 cards are Goblin creatures; Rabblemaster, Searslicer, Battle Cry Goblin, 7 burn | 2.05 |
| A Troop of Dwarves | 0 | 20 / 6 | a Treasure whenever a Dwarf enters | 12 Dwarves, 13 Equipment (11 make Hero tokens), 3 Raise the Alarm, Shock, Suplex | 2.48 |
| The Restless Dead | 2 | 20 / 7 | a 2/2 Zombie whenever a nontoken creature of theirs dies | 29 Zombies, 3 lords or anthems, recursion, self-mill, 4 removal | 3.02 |
| Krenko's Warband (boss) | last | 30 / 7, commander Krenko, Mob Boss | Goblins +1/+0, a 1/1 Goblin each combat | 48 creatures (all Goblins), 12 burn, token makers; 60 cards | 2.37 |

How each plays (from the lists and the logs I read; *likely*):
- **Goblin Raiders**: a swarm that curves out. 12 copies at MV 1 and 16 at MV 2, 33 Goblins, double strike and menace bodies, Battle Cry Goblin's {1}{R} pump with haste, Rabblemaster and Searslicer tokens, seven burn spells to clear blockers. It starts with 6 cards but has almost no late game, so its plan is to end the game around turn 5 or 6 of its own. Against a hero that spends turns 1 to 3 on 1/1 flyers and 2/1s, a one-for-one counterspell or bounce loses tempo to a stream of cheap bodies.
- **A Troop of Dwarves**: slow midrange. 24 copies at MV 2, no printed evasion (only what Dragoon's Lance gives, flying on its turn, and Samurai's Katana, trample), Equipment that costs 4 or 5 to move, and 11 Job-select cards that make their own Hero bodies. Games last about 19 turns, the longest of any enemy, which is why it is the most beatable and also the match where hero decking and attrition come into play.
- **The Restless Dead**: a grind. Cheap Zombies, recursion (three Gravedigger, two Reassembling Skeleton), self-mill, and four removal spells; average MV 3.0 with 12 copies at MV 4. Open Graves makes it resilient to trades. It is the only enemy whose deck is meant to win a long game.
- **Krenko's Warband**: the Raiders plan with seven more cards: Beetleback Chief, General Kreat, Dropkick Bomber (a second anthem), Goblin Goliath, Krenko, Tin Street Kingpin, three more burn, plus Krenko, Mob Boss from the command zone and a free token every turn. Thirty life and a 7-card hand.

Observations (certain):
- Row gating is the only ladder: rows 0 and 1 draw from {Raiders, Dwarves}; rows 2 to 6 from {Raiders, Dwarves, Dead} with equal odds (`code/RunMap.java:92-104`). There is no scaling by row (dropped by decision 19) and no rule against repeats. On a random path the hero meets Raiders 1.69 times, Dwarves 1.69 times and Dead 0.92 times on average; 87% of random paths contain at least one Raiders fight.
- The first fight is always a Raiders or Dwarves fight (row 0 is all enemies). With 1, 2 or 3 start nodes (6%, 56%, 38% of maps) the chance that every start is Raiders is about 22%.
- The two row-0 enemies are far apart in strength (section 1.6: Raiders 8%, Dwarves 44% hero wins). The only ladder signal, the Dead's row gate and bigger hand, places a middle-difficulty enemy (23%) later, while the hardest normal enemy is available on row 0.
- Raiders and Krenko are nearly the same deck: 15 of Raiders' 16 card names are in the boss list (38 of 40 copies), and all of Raiders' Base text ("Goblins have haste") is a subset of the boss's Goblin theme. For a player the boss's difference is Warren plus Krenko plus 10 extra life.
- The boss kills quickly: 8 to 10 total turns on average, which is 4 to 5 turns each, while the hero has 4 or 5 lands. With 35 of 60 cards at MV 2 or less and a free token each turn, it is hard to see how a starter deck can win this; every sweep and report agrees.

Gimmick versus deck (cohesion):
- Raiders: Base and deck agree. The haste adds little because Fanatical Firebrand has haste, Battle Cry Goblin grants it, and Rabblemaster's tokens have it.
- Dwarves: the Base only touches 12 of 40 cards; the deck's real theme is Equipment (13 cards), which the Base does not support. Hero tokens from Job select are not Dwarves, so the 11 Job-select cards never make Treasures. Not a defect, a thin link.
- Restless Dead: the best match. Open Graves makes Zombie tokens, and 29 Zombies plus Lord of the Accursed, Cemetery Reaper and Liliana's Mastery pump them; Reassembling Skeleton, Hungry Ghoul (sacrifice) and Gravedigger all trigger the Base. The two mill creatures (Crow of Dark Tidings, Wailing Ghoul: 6 copies mill 2, Crow mills again on death) feed Crypt Feaster's threshold and Gravedigger, but also thin the enemy's own 40-card library in long games (guess: not seen in sims).
- Krenko: Warren (Goblins +1/+0, a 1/1 Goblin each combat on its turn) matches a deck of Goblins and Krenko's "T: make X Goblins". Two Krenkos (commander plus Krenko, Tin Street Kingpin, two copies) and General Kreat (legendary, two copies) risk the occasional legend-rule loss (minor).

Observations and concerns:
- *Certain:* no Base script silently does nothing or something other than its text. The filters are right (`Creature.!token+YouCtrl`, `Creature.token+equipped+YouCtrl`, `Creature.IsCommander+YouOwn`).

### 2.5 Interactions with this mode's rules

- **No lands and the 10-land cap.** Every activated Base ability costs generic mana ({2}, {3}), so they are real sinks and scale well to the cap; none cares about lands. Mountain Pass Treasures are not lands, so they add mana above the cap (the mode already uses Treasures as overflow for ramp).
- **Commander always available.** Sunforger, War Drum and Stagger lean on this. The tax (+2 per recast) limits a 3 or 4 cost commander to four casts per game unless Treasures help.
- **Tokens.** Open Graves ignores tokens (fixed). Goblin Warren and Whetstone pump tokens. Armory gives haste to equipped tokens only, which in Lightning's deck means the four Job-select cards.
- **Equipment.** Armory targets any Equipment (Sunforger included); Bodhum counts only creatures that are equipped; neither helps the Dwarves deck's 13 Equipment (the enemy has no Equipment Base).
- **Base is outside normal interaction.** None of the heroes' cards can remove or counter a Base or its tokens trigger, which is by design (DESIGN.md 3.4). It does mean Warren's engine can only be raced, not answered.

## 3. Upgrades

### 3.1 Where upgrades come from

| Source | How it works (code) | Per run |
|---|---|---|
| Card-reward options | Each single-card option for a creature has a 25% chance of a random pool entry (`code/Run.java:446-449`, `content/config.json:46`). Noncreature options and bucket cards never get one | see below |
| Upgrade node | Offers 3 (creature, upgrade) pairs from the commander plus every creature name in the deck; take one or skip (`code/Run.java:491-511`) | 0.60 nodes on a random path, 1.11 at best |
| Shop | None | 0 |
| Meta unlocks | Jill and Lightning: commander +1/+1 at L6 (2,280 XP) and Ward 1 at L10 (7,880 XP) (`content/characters/*.json`) | 0 until L6 |
| Pool (`content/upgrades.json`) | +1/+1, +1/+0, +0/+2, Haste, Vigilance, Trample, Lifelink, Ward 1, each equally likely | |

### 3.2 Expected numbers

I computed these from the code and the pools (approximate for the Full Pool, whose creature share I took from the Forge set files for FIN and FDN in the run's colours, about 46% to 54%).

- Share of single-card options that are creatures: curated slot about 78% (`blue_core`), 66% (`uw_core`), 68% (`ub_core`), 61% (`frozen_core`), 42% (`rw_core`); Full Pool slot about 46% to 54%. One slot is always curated, one always Full Pool, the third is either (`code/Run.java:421-426`).
- Expected upgraded options on a single-card reward screen: mono-blue 0.46, UW 0.44, UB 0.45, Frozen 0.40, Lightning 0.36. Chance that at least one of the three options is upgraded: 40%, 38%, 39%, 35%, 32%.
- Single-card reward screens are 70% of rewards (`bucketRewardChance` 0.3, and only when buckets exist; buckets never carry upgrades).
- A hero that wins every fight on a random path (4.3 rewards) sees about 3.0 single-card screens. If they always take an upgraded option when one is offered, that is about 1.2 upgrades (mono-blue) or 1.0 (Lightning). Adding Upgrade nodes (0.6 random, up to 1.1 when seeking) gives about **1.8 to 2.3 upgrades for Jill and 1.6 to 2.1 for Lightning** in a full run. A player who picks among the three options at random gets about 0.5 from rewards.
- In the third report the simulated runs averaged 2.3 to 3.1 matches, so the real number there is well under one per run (5 Upgrade nodes entered in the 20 runs of batch B, 6 in batch C, none in A; the greedy policy values an upgraded option at only +1 score).
- Unlock-installed upgrades: none before L6, one at L6, two at L10, all on the commander.

### 3.3 Strength

- Per upgrade, the average gain is about 1.7 stat points when it is a power or toughness upgrade (3 of 8 entries), otherwise a keyword. A +1/+1 on a 2-copy 2/1 is a real change (a 50% bigger body); the same on a 4/3 is not.
- Entries of doubtful value (*likely*): Haste on creatures with flash or an ETB payoff (most of Jill's); Vigilance on a defensive deck; Trample on small flyers or on Lightning (who has it); Lifelink on Lightning (who has it). 25% of rolls on Lightning's commander are already on her card (Trample, Lifelink); Hraesvelgr (Vigilance, Ward 2) can roll a dead Vigilance. Nothing checks for duplicates (`code/Run.java:507`).
- Strong entries: Lifelink on an evasive creature (Skyship Buccaneer 4/3 flyer), Ward 1 against the red decks' burn, +1/+1 on a cheap body that is already good.
- Upgrades stack without limit (per name, additive). The Upgrade node can offer the same name again, so a favourite 2-drop can reach +3/+3 over several nodes. Unlock-installed upgrades arrive at fixed levels only.
- Not reachable at all: spells, Equipment, and anything made as a token (Hero, Thopter, Zombie, Goblin). In Lightning's deck 9 of 16 names (56%) can never be upgraded; in mono-blue 8 of 16.
- Compared with a card reward: a reward adds two copies of a new card; an upgrade changes one name. A typical full run's 2 upgrades is small next to 4 or 5 reward picks, which is likely intended but means "upgrades" are a minor system today.
- DESIGN.md D4 asked which upgrade kinds are in scope (the engine can also do abilities, mana cost, colours, types). Built: power/toughness and keywords only (`code/RunUpgrade.java`).
- DESIGN.md N10 says "reward cards come upgraded 25% of the time". The code gives 25% to creature options only, so the effective share is 11% to 19% of single-card options (curated 78% creatures, Full Pool about 46%). This is a text/code mismatch, not a bug.

## 4. Nodes, map and economy

### 4.1 Node types and weights

Node types: Enemy, Shop, Rest, Cut, Upgrade, Boss (`code/NodeType.java`). Row 0 is always Enemy; the Boss is the single last node reached from every node of the last row (`code/RunMap.java:27-65`). Rows 1 to 6 draw each node's type independently from the weights, which sum to 1.0 (`content/config.json:30-34`, `code/RunMap.java:75-90`). Seven rows, four columns, three paths (`config.json:27-29`).

| Type | Weight | Expected per random path (7 nodes, row 0 included) | Best path on average | Share of maps with at least one | What it does (code) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Enemy | 0.55 (row 0: 1.0) | 4.30 | 5.29 | 100% | A real match; +25 gold, 30 XP, a card reward (`Run.java:213-227`) |
| Shop | 0.12 | 0.73 | 1.30 | 82% | 3 relics at 60, 3 cards (2 copies) at 30, card removal 75/150/225 (`Run.java:466-489`) |
| Rest | 0.13 | 0.78 | 1.39 | 85% | Heal 30% of run max life (6 of 20), `Run.java:128-133` |
| Cut | 0.10 | 0.60 | 1.11 | 76% | Free removal of every copy of one card name, skippable (`Run.java:513-521`, `cutCopies` 0 = all) |
| Upgrade | 0.10 | 0.60 | 1.11 | 77% | 3 random (creature, upgrade) pairs, pick one or skip (`Run.java:491-511`) |
| Boss | last | 1 | 1 | 100% | The only boss, Krenko; ends the run on a win |

Method: a Python replica of the generator read from `RunMap.java`; Java behaves the same by reading, but I did not run the Java generator. Fights before the boss: 1: 0.8%, 2: 6%, 3: 19%, 4: 30%, 5: 28%, 6: 14%, 7: 3% (binomial on a random path, row 0 included).

### 4.2 What the player can actually reach

- A map has about 16 nodes (2.3 per row), 1 to 3 start nodes (6% / 56% / 38%) and on average 7.6 distinct start-to-boss paths (range 2 to 39).
- At 66% of steps the player has exactly one option; 28% have two and 6% three. A random-walk path has 2.4 steps with a real choice out of seven. The player sees node types before choosing, so they can steer toward a Rest or Shop when the fork offers one, but most forks do not.
- Real agency is therefore about "which of two or three paths", not planning a build. Concern (*likely*): this limits the roguelike feel (choose Shop or Rest at a fork) and makes the run depend on the roll.
- Constraints and absences: no guaranteed Rest or Shop; the last row before the boss is a Rest on only 13% of its nodes; 15% of maps have no Rest anywhere, 18% no Shop, 24% no Cut, 23% no Upgrade; Cut and Upgrade can land directly before the boss. No Elite, Event or Treasure node exists (D8 is open in DESIGN.md; account unlocks for more node types are "not built" per 6.5.1).
- All six node types are reachable on at least three quarters of maps, so no type is dead; none is guaranteed.

### 4.3 Nodes with unclear or overlapping roles

- **Cut node versus shop card removal.** Cut is free, always lists every card name and is skippable. The shop sells the same effect for 75 gold (then 150, 225). Both remove all copies. A second shop removal (150, so 225 with the first) exceeds the 175 gold of an all-enemy path (200 with the L4 bonus).
- **Rest versus healing relics.** Rest is 6 life; Healing Herb is worth several times that per fight.
- **Upgrade node versus reward upgrades versus meta upgrades.** Three sources of one effect; only the node lets the player choose both card and upgrade (from three).
- **Boss relic and boss gold.** With `floors` 1 the relic offered after the boss and the 100 gold are never used (`Run.java:243-259`); XP is the only boss reward that matters today.
- **Deck growth.** Every reward adds two copies of a card (or three single cards from a bucket), so a hero that wins 4 to 5 fights goes from 32 to about 40 cards unless it cuts; the thinning tools are Cut (0.6 per path) and the shop (0.73 visits). The third report's final deck sizes were 33 to 35 because runs were short.
- **Buckets versus single cards.** 30% chance of a bucket reward; buckets give 3 random single copies of cards (repeats allowed: with 6 to 7 cards in the pool, 40% to 45% of buckets contain a repeated card), no upgrades.

### 4.4 Healing and retries

| Source | Amount | Availability |
|---|---|---|
| Rest node | 30% of run max (6 at 20) | 0.78 per random path |
| Healing Herb | about +5 to +10 per fight | shop, 60 gold |
| Iron Heart | +5 max and +5 now | shop, 60 gold |
| In-match lifegain (Charming Prince, Moment of Craving, Sephiroth's Intervention, Skymarch Bloodletter, Lightning's lifelink) | counts only up to run max at the end of the fight | deck dependent |
| Meta | +2 max life at L2, +3 at L9 (Jill and Lightning) | XP |
| Retry | restores life to what it was entering the match | 1 per run (+1 at L7) |

Observations:
- Third report, Jill greedy: average life 18.7 to 6.6 over won Dwarves fights and 20.0 to 3.5 over won Raiders fights (14 and 2 won fights). Lightning ended higher than she started on average against Dwarves (lifelink). A second fight at 6 or 7 life without a Rest is dangerous for Jill.
- A retry restores to the life before the match, so a second attempt starts exactly as the first did; and quitting the app during a match lets the player replay with no penalty (DESIGN.md N12).
- With a per-fight win rate p and one retry, ignoring attrition, the chance to survive N fights is p^N (1 + N(1 - p)). For 5 fights: p = 0.5 gives 11%, 0.6 gives 23%, 0.7 gives 42%, 0.8 gives 66%. A run needs roughly 75% per fight to succeed half the time, and the boss is much harder than the rest.

## 5. Cohesion across systems

Contradictions or mismatches (each is an observation unless marked):

1. **Upgrade chance** (DESIGN.md N10 versus code): see 3.3.
2. **Boss reward is vestigial** (DESIGN.md 2.1 "big reward ... deferred"): gold and relic after the final boss have no effect with `floors` 1.
3. **Cut node versus shop removal**: two ways to do one thing at very different prices (4.3).
4. **The Frozen Throne milestone** (`content/characters/jill.json:110-123`, counter in `code/meta/MatchStatsTracker.java`): needs 30 bounces of the boss's commander by Jill's ETB, and only a boss has a commander, so it only counts in boss fights. A run meets the boss at most once, and the boss was reached once in 50 simulated runs. Jill can be cast about four times per game, and her trigger must hit Krenko on the battlefield. At 1 to 3 per boss fight this needs 10 to 30 boss fights. A human win rate would need to be far above the simulated one. (*Likely*; I read the counter code but did not run it.) The thematically matching Base (Frozen Bastion, L5) is on a separate track from the starter (milestone), and the starter itself comes with Wayfarer's Camp.
5. **Meta pacing against win rate** (section 1 summary item 5): XP table.

   | Character level | XP needed in total | Equivalent fights at 30 XP |
   |---|---|---|
   | 2 | 30 | 1 |
   | 3 | 180 | 6 |
   | 4 | 680 | 23 |
   | 5 | 1,380 | 46 |
   | 6 | 2,280 | 76 |
   | 8 | 4,680 | 156 |
   | 10 | 7,880 | 263 |

   Account levels need 500, 1,500, 3,000, 5,000, 7,500, 10,500, 14,000, 18,000 XP in total for levels 2 to 9 (`meta.json`, base 500 and +500 each step). A won run is about 780 XP (4.3 fights x 30 + 150 + 500). Simulated runs earned 9 to 33 XP, so a character's L3 takes about 6 to 20 runs and account level 2 about 15 to 55. If wins become normal these numbers drop by an order of magnitude; the designer should decide which rate they are tuning for.
6. **Green Ramp bucket** (account level 3): its colour identity is green, so it is offered only as the occasional "(splash)" slot (30% bucket reward x 25% splash x 1 in 5 off-colour buckets for Jill, 1 in 3 for Lightning: 1.5% and 2.5% per reward, about 6% and 10% per run).
7. **Buckets by colour**: mono-blue and UB runs have two on-colour buckets (Back to the Hand, Wings!) until the Through the Ages bucket arrives at account level 4; no bucket contains black cards, and the only green bucket is the locked one. A bucket reward for such a run always shows the same two, plus an occasional splash. The reward-pool design assumed three per offer (`bucketRewardChoices` 3).
8. **Base unlocks that replace instead of add** (Frozen Bastion, Bodhum Beachfront): see 2.2.
9. **Simulations and the unlock tracks**: none of the three existing reports runs with a profile, so every number reflects a fresh account (no extra life, no Sunforger, no upgrade, no unlocked relics). `RunSimulator.simulateEncounter` always starts a bare `Run.start` (`code/sim/RunSimulator.java:147`). Lightning's simulated strength at the start is therefore a lower bound on her strength after L3.
10. **The AI does not use the Armory** (0 activations in 8 logged games, Equipment cast in 5; section 2.2): all Lightning simulations understate Lightning for a human player. I did not trace why in the AI code; the same script shape in the card Brass Squire carries `AI:RemoveDeck:All`, which suggests the AI cannot use it.
11. **Reward pools**: `frozen_core` (Frozen Throne, "independent of the core") contains Counterspell, a Jill core card. The independence rule applies only to the starter deck, so this is allowed, but it means an "independent" run can be offered a card from the core. Draft pools contain out-of-scope cards (known; 92 of 411).
12. **Dwarves' gimmick** touches 12 of 40 cards while its deck theme is Equipment (1.5).
13. **Raiders and Krenko duplication** (1.5): four enemies in total and two of them share 38 of 40 card copies.
14. **Built but unused or thin**: multi-floor logic (`floors` > 1), Base life and hand modifiers, `cardScopeExceptions`, `xpMultiplier`, and the `pool` unlock for a non-bucket pool (no such locked pool exists). The `setToggle` unlocks (account levels 7 and 9) work but have little to switch: the account's Full Pool holds at most five sets (FIN, FDN, FCA, FIC, FDC), of which two can be dropped.
15. **Design aspirations not built** (DESIGN.md says so, listed for completeness): Base growth (B3), swapping Bases at a node, a "play Shiva" relic, floors, extra node types, run difficulty, more than one boss.

What I did not check: the overrides (landless rewrites of 32 cards) and the rest of the reward pools beyond the colours and creature share; the Android build; human play and the phone UI; how the AI uses Frozen Bastion; the Full Pool's actual contents (I approximated them from set files); how a human pilot compares with the AI results above (a guess: better, particularly with reactive cards, as the third report also says).
